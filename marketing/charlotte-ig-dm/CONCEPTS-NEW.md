# New concepts — Charlotte free photo shoot (22 Oct 2026)

Concepts only; Aidan generates. Every one is judged on two things: **stop** (does card 1 stop the thumb) and **click** (is there a reason to tap Send message). Rules kept: no drawn buttons/notifications/checkboxes (Meta), no film talk, no job/casting words, text never on a face, city huge where there is a city.

## A. Shot in Charlotte (the strongest lever you have not used: the real city)
1. **"Can I take your photo?"** — POV video walking up to a stranger in NoDa/South End, the ask, the shot, the shot on screen. Stop: people cannot not watch a stranger get photographed. Click: it shows exactly what happens. Needs: a phone filmed by a second person, 2–3 strangers.
2. **The A-frame.** A real sidewalk sign on a Charlotte street: "FREE PHOTO SHOOT →" with the arrow pointing at whoever walks past; card 2 is the person who stopped. Stop: a real object in a real place. Click: it is local and happening.
3. **Billboard.** The portrait composited onto a real Charlotte billboard or bus shelter, city skyline behind, "FREE" the only word. Stop: scale. Click: "this is here."
4. **Lynx car.** The portrait on the side of a Blue Line train or inside a car, Charlotte stop names visible. Stop: recognition. Click: local.
5. **Graffiti wall.** "CHARLOTTE / FREE PHOTO SHOOT" sprayed on a wall; she stands in front of it, the words beside her, never on her. Stop: colour + scale. Click: the whole offer is the backdrop.
6. **Skyline window.** Portrait shot against the Uptown skyline at dusk, the city lights the only "copy"; one line: "free photo shoot." Stop: the view. Click: "I want that photo of me."
7. **The print, held.** A hand holds a 4×6 print of her up against the same Charlotte spot it was shot in; the city behind matches the print. Stop: the trick of the eye. Click: proof it is real and local.

## B. Objects and paper
8. **Boarding pass.** CLT is the airport code. Card: a boarding pass, FROM "your camera roll" TO "CLT", SEAT 1A, FARE $0, the portrait where the barcode would be (no barcode). Stop: everyone knows the shape. Click: CLT = Charlotte, FREE.
9. **Passport page.** The portrait as a passport photo, "CHARLOTTE" as the issuing authority, "FREE PHOTO SHOOT" in the MRZ-style line. Stop: official-looking. Click: it is "yours."
10. **Parking ticket.** A Charlotte-style violation envelope under a wiper: VIOLATION — no good photos. FINE — $0.00. Stop: dread, then relief. Click: $0.
11. **Scratch card.** A lottery scratcher half-scratched, the silver revealing FREE · FREE · FREE and a portrait. Stop: the texture. Click: you already won.
12. **Dollar bill, $0.** A bill with her portrait in the oval, "ZERO DOLLARS", "CHARLOTTE" where the bank name goes. Stop: currency pulls the eye. Click: the price is the design. (Obvious parody; keep denomination 0.)
13. **Door hanger.** Hotel "DO NOT DISTURB — free photo shoot in progress", the portrait on the hanger. Stop: a thing you have held. Click: it is going on now.
14. **Vending machine.** Portraits behind the glass in rows, every slot priced $0.00, one slot empty with "you". Stop: dense + a hole. Click: the empty slot.
15. **Fortune cookie.** The slip reads "You will be photographed in Charlotte." Card 2: the portrait. Stop: tiny text people lean into. Click: it is about them.
16. **Calendar page.** A wall calendar for the month, the shoot dates circled in red marker, the portrait as the month's photo. Needs real dates. Stop: red circles. Click: a deadline.
17. **Red string board.** Portraits pinned to cork, red string running from each to one empty pin labelled "you?". Stop: the detective trope. Click: the empty pin.

## C. Puzzles with no fake controls
18. **Crossword.** A filled crossword: CHARLOTTE across, FREE down, SHOOT across, the portrait in the black squares. Stop: people read grids. Click: it spells the offer.
19. **Spot the difference.** Two near-identical portraits side by side; the only difference is "$300" vs "$0" on a tag in the corner. Stop: the eye hunts. Click: it finds the price.
20. **Spelling it with people.** C-H-A-R-L-O-T-T-E, one portrait per letter but each person is *posing* the letter's shape (arms up for the T, etc.). Needs a shoot. Stop: bodies as type. Click: nine people already said yes.
21. **The hole.** A portrait with a person-shaped hole cut out of it, the hole labelled "you". Stop: negative space. Click: the ad is literally missing the viewer.

## D. Borrowed formats (none used before)
22. **YouTube thumbnail.** The most click-optimised visual language there is: big face, mouth slightly open, giant 3-word text "FREE PHOTOS, CHARLOTTE", a red arrow. No play button. Stop + click are the whole point of the format.
23. **Comic panel.** Halftone portrait, a speech bubble: "free?" — "free." Stop: the bubble. Click: the shortest possible conversation.
24. **Broadcast lower-third.** A live-sports style name bar under the portrait: AIDAN · PORTRAITS · CHARLOTTE, score box reads $0. Stop: motion graphics feel. Click: the price in the score.
25. **Horoscope.** "Libra — a stranger offers you free photos this week. Say yes." One line per sign over the portrait. Stop: people read their sign. Click: it told them to.
26. **Retro digicam back.** The LCD on the back of an early-2000s point-and-shoot shows the portrait, the only other thing on screen "FREE". Not a viewfinder; a back screen. Stop: nostalgia. Click: "I want to look like that."
27. **Stock-photo watermark.** A gorgeous portrait with a giant diagonal watermark reading FREE instead of a stock site's name. Stop: everyone knows the watermark. Click: no price, no license.
28. **Projected type.** A projector throws "CHARLOTTE" onto the wall beside her (never on her), she stands in the spill. Needs a shoot. Stop: light as type. Click: editorial.

## E. Video (short, loop-safe)
29. **One hour in five seconds.** A time-lapse of a whole session from arrival to the last frame, 5 s, the finished portrait as the last second. Stop: speed. Click: "that is all it takes."
30. **The reveal.** She looks at the back of the camera and reacts (real reaction, filmed), cut to the photo she saw. Stop: a face reacting. Click: the photo is the payoff.
31. **Camera roll vs. this.** Her actual phone selfie (ask her for one) wipes to the portrait, same person, same day. Stop: the contrast. Click: "that could be me."
32. **The walk-off.** 3 s: she walks out of frame, the empty background holds, "you?" fades in. Stop: emptiness after a person. Click: the question.

## What to make first if CPC is the only thing that matters
1 (can I take your photo), 22 (YouTube thumbnail), 8 (boarding pass, CLT), 31 (camera roll vs this), 21 (the hole), 10 (parking ticket), 2 (A-frame), 14 (vending machine). These have the biggest stop *and* a built-in reason to tap.
