Nervous about docking your boat? A calm pre-approach checklist

Most boaters find docking the hardest part, especially with wind, current or people watching. A plan made before you arrive makes a big difference. This is general information, not boating instruction; take lessons from a qualified instructor.

Before you arrive

  • Check the wind and current at your berth, and decide which side you'll come in on.
  • Rig fenders and lines on the right side before you enter the marina.
  • Decide your abort plan: where you'll go round again if the approach isn't working.

Brief your crew

  • Who handles the bow line, who handles the stern line, and who fends off.
  • Tell everyone to step, not jump, onto the dock, and never put hands or feet between the boat and the dock.
  • Everyone wears a lifejacket.

On the approach

  • Go slowly: only as fast as you need for control. Short bursts of power work better than steady throttle.
  • If it's going wrong, back out and try again; there's no prize for getting it first time.

Practise

Practise in open water using a buoy or a fender as a target, and practise at quiet times of day. Lessons with an instructor on your own boat are often the quickest way to build confidence.

Learn from each docking

Afterwards, note the conditions and what went well or wrong. Patterns, such as always drifting one way, tell you exactly what to practise.
