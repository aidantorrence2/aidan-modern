Buying a used car when you know nothing about cars

You don't need to be a mechanic to avoid most bad used cars. A checklist, some patience and a willingness to walk away do most of the work.

Before you look

  • Set a total budget, including insurance, tax, fuel and a buffer for repairs.
  • Pick two or three models known for reliability and read owner reviews of common faults.

At the viewing

  • View in daylight and in dry weather. Check the tyres, look for mismatched paint or panel gaps, and look under the car for leaks.
  • Start the engine from cold if you can, and take a test drive that includes some faster roads.

Check the paperwork

  • Service history, ownership documents and past test or inspection records. Mileage should fit the age and records.
  • A vehicle history check can show outstanding finance, write-offs or mileage problems.

Get an inspection

  • For anything beyond a cheap runaround, pay an independent mechanic or inspection service to look at it before you buy.

Be ready to walk away

There's always another car. If the seller rushes you, won't let you inspect it, or the story doesn't add up, say thanks and leave. Check your consumer rights where you live, which differ for dealers and private sellers.
