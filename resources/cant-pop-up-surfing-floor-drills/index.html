Can't pop up when surfing? Floor drills to practise at home

Getting from lying down to standing in one smooth move is the hardest part of learning to surf for most adults. Practising on dry land, where you can repeat it dozens of times, speeds things up a lot. This is general information; take lessons from a qualified instructor and surf safely.

Break it into steps

  • Lie on the floor in your paddling position, chest up.
  • Place your hands flat under your chest, not on the edges of an imaginary board.
  • Push up and bring your back foot through first, then your front foot between your hands.
  • Land low, knees bent, looking forward, not down.

Drill little and often

  • Ten minutes a day beats an hour once a week.
  • Do each step slowly until it's easy, then join them together.
  • Then practise the whole pop-up at speed.

Check yourself on video

  • Film from the side on your phone.
  • Look for: hands under your chest, back foot landing first, feet across the board, eyes forward.
  • Fix one thing at a time.

In the water

  • Pop up a fraction earlier, as the board starts to move with the wave.
  • Keep your weight centred so you don't nose-dive or stall.
  • Note after each session how many times you got to your feet and what went wrong.

Stay safe

Surf at lifeguarded beaches between the flags where you can, check conditions and tides, and stay in waves that suit your ability. If you have back, shoulder or heart problems, check with a doctor first.
