Learning the clawhammer banjo stroke on your own: bum-ditty, step by step

The basic clawhammer pattern, often called 'bum-ditty', takes most people weeks to feel natural. Slow, short and regular practice gets there.

The three parts

  • Bum: strike down on one string with the back of your fingernail.
  • Ditty: brush down across the strings, then catch the 5th string with your thumb.
  • The thumb doesn't pluck so much as rest on the 5th string and release it as the hand comes up.

Go slow with a metronome

  • Set a slow tempo and play only the pattern on open strings.
  • Raise the tempo a little only when it feels even several times in a row.

Stay loose

  • Let the motion come from a loose wrist and forearm, like shaking water off your hand.
  • If you tense up, stop, shake out the hand, and start again slower.

Record yourself

  • A quick phone recording shows whether the beats are even. It's easier to hear on playback than while playing.

Move to a tune

Once the stroke holds, pick a simple tune and play its melody notes with the same motion. Little and often (10 to 15 minutes a day) beats one long weekly session.
