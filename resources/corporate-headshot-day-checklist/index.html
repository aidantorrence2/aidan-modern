Corporate headshot day checklist: what to send HR before you arrive

Headshot days are won or lost before the first frame: the right room, the right slot length, and one clear email to staff. This is the checklist I run with the HR contact two weeks out. Copy it into your own booking confirmation.

Two weeks out: agree these with HR

  • Headcount, and whether leadership gets longer slots or a second look.
  • Slot length: ten minutes a person for a standard look with a quick review, fifteen if they pick their frame on the spot. Add a ten-minute buffer every hour.
  • The room: at least four metres long, blinds or no windows, a power socket, and no through traffic. A boardroom beats a lobby.
  • Look: background colour, framing (head and shoulders or waist up), dress note, and whether glasses stay on.
  • Who owns the schedule: you send a booking link, or HR fills a sheet. Decide now; both at once ends in double bookings.
  • Delivery: who picks the final frame, what retouching is included, file sizes, naming convention (firstname-lastname), and the deadline.
  • Invoice terms and any purchase order number, so payment does not wait on paperwork after the fact.

The email HR forwards to staff (copy this)

Subject: Headshots on [date], book your 10-minute slot

Hi all,

[Photographer] is in the office on [date] from [start] to [end] for new headshots. Book a slot here: [link]. It takes about ten minutes.

Wear what you would wear to meet a client; solid colours photograph best, and avoid fine stripes or large logos. Glasses are fine. Please arrive two minutes early.

You will get a link to choose your favourite frame within [n] days of the session.

[HR name]

The day before

  • Confirm the room is still yours and the slots are full; send the roster to HR with gaps marked so they can fill them.
  • Charge everything, pack a backup body, and bring a small mirror and a lint roller for the table by the door.
  • Print the roster. Phones die; paper does not.

On the day

  • Set up thirty minutes early and shoot a test frame of the HR contact; it checks the look and gives them a photo to send round as a nudge.
  • Tick people off as they arrive; anyone who misses their slot goes to a short waitlist at the end rather than being squeezed in.
  • Say the same three directions to everyone, in the same order. Consistency across eighty people is the product.
  • Before packing up, give HR a count: shot, no-shows, and who still needs a session.

After

  • Send pick links within the promised window, one per person, with a deadline. Unchosen picks after the deadline default to your choice; say so up front.
  • Deliver in one named folder to HR, plus the roster with status per person: picked, retouched, delivered.
  • Ask HR for the dates of their next intake. Headshot days recur; the second booking should be a reply, not a pitch.
