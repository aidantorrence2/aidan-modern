Scared of lifts? Small steps that help many people with claustrophobia

A fear of lifts is common, and avoiding them usually makes the fear stronger over time. Many people improve with gradual, repeated practice. This is general information, not therapy; if your fear is severe or causes panic attacks, talk to a GP.

How gradual practice works

You start with a step that makes you a little uneasy, stay with it until the unease drops, and repeat it before moving to the next step. Over time your body learns the situation is safe.

An example ladder

  • Watch a lift open and close from a distance.
  • Stand by the open doors, then step in and out while someone holds them.
  • Ride one floor with someone you trust, then one floor alone.
  • Ride several floors, then at a busier time.

Calming in the moment

  • Slow breathing: breathe in for 4, out for 6, for a minute or two.
  • Notice what you can see and hear, and remind yourself the feeling will pass.
  • Rate your unease from 0 to 10 before and after; seeing it fall helps.

Stay safe

Only practise in normal, working public lifts. Never force doors or use a lift that's out of order.

Get help if you need it

CBT (cognitive behavioural therapy) is an effective treatment for phobias. In the UK you can often refer yourself to NHS talking therapies, or ask your GP.
