How to find your property corner pins without a surveyor

Most US lots have survey markers at their corners, often iron rods or pipes set by a surveyor. You can often find them yourself. Finding a pin doesn't settle a boundary legally, but it helps you plan.

Start with your records

  • Look in your closing documents for a survey or plat.
  • If there isn't one, ask your county recorder or land records office for the recorded plat of your subdivision.
  • Note the distances and angles between corners.

Find the first pin

  • Start at a corner that's easy to reach, often near the street.
  • Pins are usually metal rods or pipes, sometimes with a plastic cap, and can be a few inches below the surface.
  • A basic metal detector helps; probe gently and dig only a little by hand.

Measure to the next one

  • From a pin you've found, measure the distance on the plat toward the next corner with a long tape.
  • Search around where you end up; small differences are normal.

Rules and safety

  • Call 811 before digging anywhere, so buried utilities are marked.
  • Never move or remove a survey marker; in many places that's against the law.
  • Hire a licensed land surveyor for fences on the line, disputes with neighbours, or pins you can't find.
