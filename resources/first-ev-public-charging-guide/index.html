Public charging for your first electric car: a plain-English starter guide

Public charging looks confusing because there are many networks, apps and charger types. A little setup before your first long trip makes it much easier.

Know your connectors

  • Most newer cars in the UK and Europe use a Type 2 socket for slower AC charging and CCS for rapid DC charging. Some older cars use CHAdeMO. Check your car's manual.

Set up payment once

  • Many rapid chargers now take contactless bank cards. It's still worth setting up one or two network apps or a multi-network card or app before you travel, in case a charger doesn't.

Understand charging speed

  • Rapid charging is fastest at lower battery levels and slows a lot above about 80%, so on trips it's usually quicker to charge to around 80% and move on.

Plan a main and a backup stop

  • Pick a charging stop with several chargers and a backup nearby, and check live status in a map app before you leave the motorway.

Practise locally

Try a public charger near home on a quiet day, so your first public charge isn't at a busy services. Note overstay fees, which some networks charge if you stay plugged in after charging finishes.
