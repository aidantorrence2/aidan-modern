Horse won't load into the trailer? Calm steps to try (and when to get help)

A horse that won't load is stressful, especially on show or vet day. Pressure and force tend to make it worse and can get people and horses hurt. This is general information; for a horse that is dangerous to load, work with an experienced trainer.

Stay safe

  • Wear a hat, gloves and sturdy boots, and have a helper.
  • Never stand directly behind the horse or in the path of a horse that might rush back out.
  • Stop if either of you is getting upset, and finish on a calm moment.

Check the trailer first

  • Look at the floor and ramp for soft spots, gaps or slipperiness.
  • Check for wasps, sharp edges and anything rattling or loose.
  • Make it light and open inside: open the front, move the divider across if you can.

Practise when you don't need to go anywhere

  • Break loading into small steps: walking past, standing at the ramp, one foot on, front feet on, all the way in.
  • Reward each small step and stop while it's going well.
  • Note how far you got each time, so you can see progress across sessions.

If he used to load fine

A sudden change can have a physical cause, such as pain, so talk to your vet. Think about what changed: a new trailer, a scary journey, a different driver or a new yard.

Get help

If your horse rears, pulls back hard or rushes out, get help from a qualified trainer rather than trying harder on your own.
