Service charge gone up with no breakdown? How leaseholders can question it (England and Wales)

Service charges can rise sharply, and it isn't always clear what you're paying for. Leaseholders in England and Wales have rights to information and to challenge charges that aren't reasonable. This is general information, not legal advice.

Start with your lease

  • Your lease sets out what can be charged for and how costs are shared.
  • Check what it says about the reserve fund, management fees and admin charges.

Ask for information in writing

  • You can ask your landlord or managing agent in writing for a summary of the costs that make up the service charge.
  • You can then ask to inspect the invoices and receipts behind it.
  • Keep a copy of every request and note the date you sent it.

Compare year by year

  • Put each year's budget and accounts side by side.
  • Note lines that jumped, lines with no visible work, and new lines with no explanation.
  • Talk to neighbours: a residents' association has more weight than one leaseholder.

If you can't resolve it

  • Use the managing agent's complaints process, and its redress scheme if it belongs to one.
  • Service charges must be reasonable. In England you can apply to the First-tier Tribunal (Property Chamber); in Wales, the Leasehold Valuation Tribunal.
  • Before applying, get free initial advice from LEASE (the Leasehold Advisory Service) or Citizens Advice.
