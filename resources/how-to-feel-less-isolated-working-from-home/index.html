How to feel less isolated when you work from home

Working from home removes the small, casual contact of an office: the chat at the kettle, lunch with a colleague, a nod on the way in. Adding a few regular, low-effort contacts back makes a real difference.

Work around people sometimes

  • Work from a café, library or coworking space one or two days a week.
  • Go to the same place at the same time, so faces become familiar.

Build in small daily contact

  • Take a walk at the same time each day and say hello to people you pass.
  • Chat to the same shop or café staff regularly.
  • Call or voice-message a friend instead of only texting.

Make work more social

  • Suggest short, optional video chats with colleagues that aren't about work.
  • Meet colleagues who live nearby in person now and then.

Add a weekly regular

  • Join something that meets every week: a class, club, team or volunteering group.
  • Put it in your calendar like a meeting so it doesn't slip.

If loneliness feels heavy

If you feel low for a long time, talk to your doctor or a support line; loneliness is common, and help is available.
