Off the wall: how to hold a handstand without support

Most people stuck at the wall have the strength already. What they lack is a safe way to fall, a straight line, and the habit of balancing with their fingers.

Learn to bail out first

  • Practise cartwheeling or stepping out of a handstand on a soft surface until it feels automatic.
  • Once falling is safe, the fear of trying drops a lot.

Stack the line

  • Against the wall, face the wall (chest to wall) rather than your back, and push tall through your shoulders.
  • Squeeze your legs together and tuck the ribs in so your body is one straight line.

Balance with your fingers

  • When you start to tip over, press your fingertips into the floor. When you fall back down, press into the heels of your hands.

Short, frequent practice

  • Ten minutes a day of short attempts beats one long session.
  • Log your best hold time each day and film yourself once a week.

Look after yourself

Warm up your wrists and shoulders first. If you have wrist, shoulder or neck problems, or high blood pressure, ask a qualified professional before starting. This guide is general information, not medical advice.
