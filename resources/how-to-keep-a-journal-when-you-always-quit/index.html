How to keep a journal when you always quit after a few weeks

Most journals are abandoned for the same reasons: entries get too long, the blank page feels like homework, and one missed week turns into quitting. Making it smaller and easier to restart fixes most of that.

Make entries tiny

  • Aim for two minutes or three sentences. Longer is fine, but not required.
  • Bullet points count. So does a single line about the day.

Answer one question

  • Instead of a blank page, answer one question: What took up most of my energy today? What am I looking forward to? What do I want to remember?
  • Keep a short list of questions you like and rotate them.

Attach it to something you already do

  • Write right after something that happens every day: morning coffee, getting into bed, the commute home.
  • Keep the journal, or the app, exactly where that moment happens.

Drop the streak

Missing days is normal. When you come back, do not try to catch up on everything you missed; just write about today. A journal with gaps is still a journal.

Look back sometimes

Once a month, read a few old entries. Seeing what changed is the reward that keeps many people going. If journaling brings up difficult feelings, consider talking to a therapist; if you are in crisis, in the US call or text 988.
