How to see the ISS or a Starlink train with your own eyes

The space station and Starlink trains are easy to see with the naked eye, if you look at the right time and in the right place.

When to look

  • Most visible passes happen shortly after sunset or before sunrise, when the sky is dark but the satellite is still lit by the sun.
  • Starlink trains are brightest in the first few days after a launch; after that the satellites spread out and dim.
  • Check the weather: you need a mostly clear sky.

Pick a good pass

  • Choose passes that rise high in the sky; low passes are often lost in haze or behind buildings.
  • Note the direction it appears from and the time; passes last only a few minutes.
  • NASA's free Spot the Station service lists visible ISS passes for your location.

What you'll see

  • The ISS looks like a very bright, steady star moving smoothly across the sky, with no flashing lights.
  • A Starlink train looks like a line of dots moving one after another.
  • If it's flashing, it's probably a plane.

Why people miss it

  • Looking in the wrong direction or too late.
  • Passes listed that are technically overhead but too low or in daylight.
  • Cloud, or bright city lights; get away from streetlights if you can.
