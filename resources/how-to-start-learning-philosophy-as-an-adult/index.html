How to start learning philosophy as an adult

Many adults try to start philosophy with a famous original text, get lost, and give up. Starting with context, then short original readings, works much better.

Start with an overview

  • Read a short introduction to philosophy first, such as Nigel Warburton's 'A Little History of Philosophy' or Thomas Nagel's 'What Does It All Mean?'.
  • They give you the map before you go into the original texts.

Then read short original texts

  • Pick short, readable works first: Plato's 'Apology' or 'Euthyphro', or Bertrand Russell's 'The Problems of Philosophy'.
  • Read a summary of the argument before and after each one.
  • Many older texts are free online in translation.

Follow questions, not just names

  • Choose a question you care about: what is a good life, what can we know, is free will real?
  • Read what two or three thinkers say about it, and compare.

Use free help

  • The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy is free online and explains topics and thinkers in depth.
  • Philosophy podcasts and lecture series can explain difficult ideas aloud.

Keep it manageable

Read a little and often, write a few lines on what you understood, and don't worry if you don't get everything the first time; philosophers disagree about most of it too.
