How to stop catching an edge and start linking turns on a snowboard

Catching an edge, where the downhill edge digs in and slams you down, is the classic beginner fall. It usually comes from a flat board, weight in the wrong place or looking down. A lesson from a qualified instructor is the quickest fix; these notes help you practise between lessons.

Why it happens

  • Riding with the board flat, so the downhill edge catches the snow.
  • Leaning back or looking at your feet.
  • Changing edge too slowly on gentle slopes.

Before you link turns

  • Practise sliding on your heel edge, then your toe edge, across the slope.
  • Try the falling leaf: zigzag across the slope on one edge without turning down the hill.
  • Keep your knees bent and your weight over your front foot.

Linking turns

  • Look where you want to go; your shoulders and board follow.
  • Shift weight smoothly from one edge to the other, using your front foot to start the turn.
  • Start on very gentle terrain and build up.

Make each day count

Focus on one thing per run, and note after each lift how many turns you linked and which edge caught you. Take that list to your next lesson.

Stay safe

Wear a helmet, consider wrist guards and padded shorts, ride within your ability, and follow resort rules.
