How to stop killing your houseplants: check the soil, not the calendar

Most houseplants are lost to two things: forgetting to water, and watering too much on a fixed schedule. Both have the same fix: check the soil before you water, and let the plant tell you when.

Check before you water

  • Push a finger into the soil up to the first knuckle. If it is dry, water. If it is damp, wait.
  • Lift the pot: a dry pot feels noticeably lighter than a freshly watered one.
  • Succulents and cacti like to dry out fully; ferns and many tropicals like soil that stays slightly moist.

Water properly

  • Water until it runs out of the drainage holes, then empty the saucer.
  • Pots without drainage holes are a common cause of rot. Use a plastic nursery pot inside a decorative one.
  • Water less in winter, when growth slows.

Set reminders that adapt

  • Use a reminder to check the soil, not to water. That way you never water a plant that does not need it.
  • Group plants with similar needs together so one check covers several.
  • If you share the home, agree who waters, so plants are not watered twice or forgotten.

Match the plant to your light

Many problems blamed on watering are really light. Check the plant's label for light needs, and move it closer to a window if it is leggy or pale.
