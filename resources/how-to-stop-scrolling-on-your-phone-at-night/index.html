How to Stop Scrolling on Your Phone at Night

Night scrolling is rarely about weak willpower. Apps are built to be easy to keep using, and at the end of a long day you are tired and looking for something easy. The fix is to make the phone a little less convenient and give your evening something better to land on.

Notice what the scrolling is doing for you

Before changing anything, spend a few evenings noticing when you pick up the phone and why. Common reasons include unwinding after work, avoiding a task, filling the quiet, feeling lonely, or simply habit when you sit down on the couch.

This matters because the reason shapes the fix. If you scroll to decompress, you need another way to decompress. If you scroll because you are bored in bed, you need the phone out of the bedroom. Trying to stop without replacing what it gives you usually does not last.

Add friction to your phone

  • Move the apps you lose time on off your home screen, or into a folder on the last page.
  • Log out of social apps so getting back in takes a few extra steps.
  • Turn off non-essential notifications, especially in the evening.
  • Use the built-in screen time or digital wellbeing tools to set app limits that start at a fixed time.
  • Try grayscale mode at night. A dull screen is less inviting.
  • Set a downtime or focus mode that only lets calls and messages from chosen people through.

Change where the phone sleeps

The simplest change for many people is charging the phone outside the bedroom. If you use it as an alarm, a basic alarm clock costs little and removes the main excuse to keep it within reach.

If that feels like too big a step, start by charging it across the room instead of next to the bed. Having to get up to grab it is often enough to break the automatic reach.

The same idea works in the living room. Give the phone a home, such as a shelf or a basket by the door, where it lives for the evening. You can still check it on purpose, but it is no longer sitting in your hand by default while you watch TV or talk with someone.

Plan something to do instead

An empty evening tends to get filled by the easiest thing available. Decide in advance what you will do with that time, and set it up so it is just as easy to start: a book on the pillow, a puzzle on the table, a playlist or podcast on a speaker, a sketchbook by the couch.

It also helps to create a clear end to your day. A short wind-down routine signals that the scrolling window is closed. If you live with others, ask whether they want to try it with you. Agreeing on a shared phone-free hour makes it far easier to stick to than going it alone.

A simple evening routine

[time]  Phone goes on the charger in [room outside the bedroom]
        Set out tomorrow's clothes or bag
        Tidy one small thing (dishes, desk, entryway)
        Something calm: [book / stretch / shower / music]
        Write down anything on your mind for tomorrow
        Lights down, bed

Be realistic about slips

You will have nights where you scroll anyway. That is normal and not a reason to abandon the plan. Notice what made that night different, such as stress, a late finish or skipping the routine, and adjust. Aim for more good evenings than before, not a perfect streak.

If you find you cannot cut back even when it is clearly costing you sleep, relationships or work, or the scrolling is tied to low mood or anxiety, it is worth talking to your doctor or a licensed therapist. They can help you look at what is underneath the habit.
