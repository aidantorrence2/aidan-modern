은/는 vs 이/가 in Korean: the questions that decide most sentences

은/는 marks the topic: what the sentence is about. 이/가 marks the subject: who or what does or is something. They often overlap, which is why it feels confusing.

Which form

  • After a consonant: 은 or 이 (책은, 책이).
  • After a vowel: 는 or 가 (저는, 제가: 저 + 가 becomes 제가).

Ask: is it new or already known?

  • New information, or answering 'who?' or 'what?': usually 이/가. 누가 왔어요? 민수가 왔어요.
  • Something already in the conversation, or a general statement about it: usually 은/는. 민수는 학생이에요.

Ask: am I contrasting?

  • 은/는 can mean 'as for X' in contrast to something else: 커피는 좋아해요 (coffee I like, unlike something else).

Patterns that usually take 이/가

  • Question words as the subject: 누가, 뭐가, 어디가.
  • 있다 and 없다: 시간이 있어요.
  • 되다 and 아니다: 의사가 되고 싶어요, 학생이 아니에요.

Practise with real sentences

Rules get you most of the way; the rest comes from seeing many examples. Read short dialogues, guess the particle before you look, and note the cases that surprise you.
