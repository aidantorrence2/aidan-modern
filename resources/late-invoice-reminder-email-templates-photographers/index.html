Late invoice reminder emails for photographers: three templates

Most brand and editorial invoices are paid late by accounts-payable departments that are not ignoring you, they are batching. The trick is to be in the batch. These three notes are short enough to send without dread. Replace the brackets and keep your own sign-off.

1. Three days before due (friendly heads-up)

Subject: Invoice [number] for [project], due [date]

Hi [name],

Quick note that invoice [number] for [project] is due on [date]. If it is already in your run, ignore this. If anything is missing on my end for processing (PO number, vendor form, W-9), tell me and I will send it today.

Thanks,
[your name]

2. The day after due (plain and specific)

Subject: Invoice [number] is now past due

Hi [name],

Invoice [number] for [amount] was due yesterday, [date]. Could you let me know when it is scheduled for payment? If it has been paid and is in transit, a remittance date is all I need.

Best,
[your name]

3. Two weeks late (firm, with your terms)

Subject: Invoice [number], 14 days overdue

Hi [name],

Invoice [number] for [amount] is now 14 days past its due date of [date]. Per the terms on the invoice, a late fee of [fee or percentage] applies from [date]; I have attached an updated copy. Please confirm a payment date by [day], or let me know who else I should be speaking to.

Regards,
[your name]

Rules that make these work

  • Send them from the same thread as the original invoice so the history is one click away.
  • Name a date in every message. 'Soon' gives them nothing to put in a system.
  • Ask who else to contact. Accounts payable often sits with a different person than your creative contact.
  • Stop the sequence the moment money lands, and say thank you. Clients remember how the end felt.
  • Only mention a late fee if it is on the invoice they already accepted.
