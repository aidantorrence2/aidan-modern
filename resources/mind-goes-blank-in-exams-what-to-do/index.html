Mind goes blank in exams even though you know the material? What to do

Lots of people know their material at their desk and then freeze when the exam starts. Rehearsing the real conditions and having a simple plan for the moment you go blank makes a big difference for many students. This is general information, not medical advice.

Rehearse the real thing

  • Do full practice papers at the same time of day as the exam, with a timer and no notes.
  • Sit somewhere unfamiliar if you can, like a library, to get used to a different room.
  • Practise the start: reading the instructions, choosing where to begin.

Have a 60-second reset

  • Put your pen down and sit back.
  • Breathe out slowly, a few times.
  • Say one short cue line to yourself, then start with the easiest part of the question.
  • Practise this in mocks so it's automatic on the day.

Don't let one question take over

  • If you're stuck, mark it, move on and come back later.
  • Jot down anything you remember about the topic; it often unlocks the rest.

The last two weeks

  • Keep your sleep, meals and exercise steady.
  • Plan the morning: what you'll eat, what you'll take, when you'll leave.

Get support

If panic attacks or anxiety are affecting your life, talk to your GP, doctor or a counsellor. Ask your college or exam body whether access arrangements are available.
