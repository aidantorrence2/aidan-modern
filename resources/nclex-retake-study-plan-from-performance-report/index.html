Failed the NCLEX? How to make a retake study plan from your performance report

Failing the NCLEX is crushing, and many nurses who pass did not pass first time. A retake goes better with a plan built from what the exam told you, rather than doing more of the same. This is general information; always check your board of nursing's retake rules.

Start with your performance report

  • If you didn't pass, you'll receive a Candidate Performance Report showing how you did in each content area.
  • List each area as below, near or above the passing standard.
  • Your weakest areas get the most study time; don't neglect the others completely.

Check the rules and dates

  • There is a waiting period before you can retake; check the current rules with your board of nursing and NCSBN.
  • Re-register and pay the fee, then pick a date that gives you enough time to prepare.

Build a day-by-day plan

  • Count the days to your retake and share them out across content areas, weighted to your weak ones.
  • Mix content review with question practice every day.
  • Read the rationale for every question, right or wrong.

Track your practice

  • Log your scores for each practice block by content area.
  • Watch for areas that stay flat, and change how you study them.
  • Take full-length practice exams in exam conditions in the final weeks.

Look after yourself

Plan rest days, keep your sleep regular in the run-up, and talk to someone you trust. If the stress feels overwhelming, speak to your GP or doctor.
