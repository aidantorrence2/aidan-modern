How to practise saying your name and making phone calls when you stammer

For many people who stammer, the hardest moments are short and everyday: saying your name, opening a phone call, ordering food. Preparing for those specific moments can make them feel more manageable. This guide is not a fluency promise; it is about feeling more in control.

Practise your own lines

  • Write down the lines you say most: your name, how you start a call, your usual order.
  • Practise them out loud when you are relaxed, using whatever techniques work for you.

Rehearse calls first

  • Practise a call with a friend, or say it aloud to yourself before you dial.
  • Have a short note in front of you with what you need to say.

Make calls on your terms

  • Choose a quiet place and a time when you feel less rushed.
  • It is fine to tell the other person you stammer and need a moment. Many people find this takes the pressure off.

Notice progress

After each call, note one thing that went well. Over time, you may notice which calls are getting easier.

Get support

A qualified speech and language therapist can help with techniques that suit you. Stammering organisations such as STAMMA in the UK offer support, information and community.
