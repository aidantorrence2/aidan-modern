Scared of cycling on busy roads? How to build up to a bike commute

Lots of people would like to cycle to work but are put off by traffic. Building up slowly on a route you know, at quiet times, and getting some proper training, helps most riders feel far more in control. This is general information, not cycle training.

Get proper training

  • In the UK, Bikeability training covers riding on roads and junctions, and many councils run adult sessions, sometimes free or low-cost.
  • Elsewhere, look for local cycle-training or bike-club beginner rides.
  • A trainer can ride your actual commute with you.

Pick a quieter route

  • It's fine to take a longer route that uses back streets, paths and cycle lanes.
  • Ride or walk it first without time pressure and note the junctions that worry you.
  • For each one, decide where you'll position yourself, or whether to get off and walk across.

Build up in steps

  • Ride the route first on a quiet Sunday morning.
  • Then try a quiet weekday time, then the real commute.
  • Move to the next step only when the last one feels manageable.

Ride assertively

  • Under the Highway Code, you can ride in the centre of the lane on quiet roads, in slower-moving traffic and at the approach to junctions, to make yourself seen.
  • Keep clear of the door zone of parked cars.
  • Never go up the inside of large vehicles at junctions.

Check your kit

Make sure your brakes, tyres and lights work, wear something easy to see, and consider a mirror or a bell. Never use your phone while riding.
