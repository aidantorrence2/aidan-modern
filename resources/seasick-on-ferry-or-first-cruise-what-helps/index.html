Worried about seasickness on a ferry or your first cruise? What helps adults

If you get seasick, a ferry crossing or a first cruise can feel like something to dread. A few choices before and during the trip make a real difference for many people. This is general information, not medical advice; a pharmacist can advise on travel sickness medicines.

Before you go

  • Ask a pharmacist about travel sickness medicines well before you travel, as many need to be taken in advance and some cause drowsiness.
  • Check the forecast and, if you can, choose a calmer crossing time.
  • Get a good night of sleep and eat a light meal before you board.

Where to be on board

  • The middle of the ship and the lower decks usually move least.
  • On a cruise, a cabin midship on a lower deck can help.
  • Get out on deck for fresh air if it is safe to do so.

While you are moving

  • Look at the horizon or a fixed point in the distance.
  • Avoid reading or looking at your phone for long periods.
  • Sip water, eat small plain snacks, and go easy on alcohol and heavy or greasy food.

In the first queasy minutes

  • Stop what you are doing and fix your eyes on the horizon.
  • Get to fresh air, or lie down and close your eyes.
  • Loosen tight clothing and take slow breaths.

Keep a note

After each trip, note where you sat, what you ate and what you did. Over a few journeys you will see what keeps you well, and you can plan the next trip around it.

When to get help

See a GP if sickness is severe, lasts long after the journey, or comes with symptoms like a bad headache, chest pain or dizziness on dry land.
