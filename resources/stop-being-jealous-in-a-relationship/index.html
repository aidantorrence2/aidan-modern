How to stop being jealous in a relationship: what to do in the moment, and after

Jealousy is a common feeling. What hurts a relationship is usually what we do next: checking, accusing, going quiet. These steps are about that next move.

In the moment: wait

  • Set a short timer before you check a phone, send a message or ask a loaded question. The urge usually peaks and then fades.
  • Breathe slowly and put the phone down. Nothing has to be decided in the next few minutes.

Story vs facts

  • Write down the thought exactly, for example 'they're losing interest'.
  • Then write only what you actually know. Often the facts are much smaller than the story.

Notice your triggers

  • Keep a short note of when it happens: late replies, social media, a name coming up, being tired or alone.
  • Patterns make it less of a surprise, and easier to plan for.

Talk when you're calm

  • Start with 'I' and the feeling: 'I felt anxious when I didn't hear from you, and I'd like to talk about it.'
  • Ask for one small, specific thing rather than a promise to never do something.

When to get more help

If jealousy fills most of your days, or you can't stop checking, a counsellor can help. If a partner checks, controls or threatens you, that is different: contact a domestic abuse helpline in your country.
