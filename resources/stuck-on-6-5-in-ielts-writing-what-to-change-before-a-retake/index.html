Stuck on 6.5 in IELTS Writing? What to change before you retake

Many test takers get the same Writing band attempt after attempt. Retaking without changing how you prepare often gives the same result. This guide is general advice and is not connected to the organisations that run the test.

Look at what's been the same each time

  • Write down every result by section, with dates.
  • Collect the essays you wrote in practice before each test.
  • Note what you did differently, if anything, between attempts.

Check your essays against the four public criteria

  • The published Writing band descriptors cover task achievement or task response, coherence and cohesion, lexical resource, and grammatical range and accuracy.
  • Rate several of your practice essays against each one, ideally with a teacher, to see which is lowest most often.
  • Task 2 counts for more of the Writing score than Task 1, so weaknesses there matter more.

Fix one thing at a time

  • Keep a list of mistakes you repeat, such as missing a part of the question, unclear paragraphs, or the same grammar errors.
  • Spend most of your practice on the weakest criterion for a few weeks.
  • Write under timed conditions so practice matches the real test.

Before you rebook

  • Check your recent practice essays are consistently meeting the level you need, not just once.
  • If you think a past result was marked wrongly, ask your test centre about a re-mark (Enquiry on Results) and its fee.
  • Some centres offer a one-skill retake for certain test types; check with your centre and with the organisation you're applying to whether it's accepted.
