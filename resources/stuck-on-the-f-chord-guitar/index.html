Stuck on the F chord? A week-by-week way past it

The F barre chord is where many adult beginners give up. It is hard at first for nearly everyone, and it gets easier with short, regular practice. The trick is to split it into small steps and keep playing music you like in the meantime.

Keep playing while you learn it

  • Use an easier version of F while you build up: the small F that only covers the top strings, or Fmaj7.
  • Pick songs you like that avoid barre chords, or play them with a capo so the shapes are easier.
  • Keep the fun part of practice going. Drills alone are how people quit.

Week 1 and 2: the index finger alone

  • Lay your index finger across all strings at the first fret and pluck each string one at a time.
  • Roll the finger slightly onto its bony side; it presses more evenly.
  • Keep your thumb low behind the neck and use the weight of your arm, not just a squeeze.

Week 3 and 4: add the shape

  • Add the other fingers to form the full F, then play it slowly, string by string, fixing any that buzz.
  • Try the same shape higher up the neck, around the fifth fret, where it is easier, then move it down.
  • Practise switching from C to F slowly: shape, strum once, release, repeat.

Make practice short and daily

Ten minutes a day beats an hour on Sunday. If your hand aches, stop for the day. Record yourself once a week; hearing the progress is what keeps most people going.

Check your guitar

High action, the height of the strings, makes barre chords much harder. A setup at a guitar shop can lower it, and lighter strings help too.
