Tiny bugs in the house: what are they and what can you do?

Finding tiny bugs at home is common and usually not dangerous. Where you find them is often the best clue. This guide is general information; for a serious or spreading problem, contact a professional pest controller or, if you rent, your landlord.

Start with where you find them

  • Bathrooms and damp corners: many tiny bugs like damp, so moisture is often the cause.
  • Kitchens and cupboards: check dry foods like flour, cereal and pet food.
  • Window sills: some tiny insects and mites gather on sunny sills in spring and summer.
  • Plants and soil: small flies and insects around houseplants are often linked to wet soil.
  • Wood: holes, dust or damage can point to wood-boring insects and may need a professional.

Look closely

  • Take a sharp, close-up photo next to a coin or ruler for scale.
  • Note the colour, size, whether it has wings, and whether it jumps, crawls or flies.
  • Note the time of year and whether there are more over time.

Simple first steps

  • Reduce damp: ventilate, fix leaks and dry wet areas.
  • Clean and vacuum regularly, including corners and skirting boards.
  • Store dry food in sealed containers and throw away anything infested.
  • Seal gaps around windows, doors and pipes.

When to get help

  • If bugs keep coming back, spread, or damage wood or fabric.
  • If you're getting bitten or have a reaction, speak to a pharmacist or doctor.
  • If you rent, tell your landlord in writing.
