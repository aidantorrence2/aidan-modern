Travel sick as a passenger? Seat, food and screen habits that help adults

Travel sickness happens when what your eyes see doesn't match the motion your body feels. Plenty of adults still get it. This is general information, not medical advice.

Where to sit

  • In a car, sit in the front passenger seat; on a coach, near the front; on a boat, in the middle and on a lower deck.
  • Face forwards, and keep your head still against the headrest.

What to look at

  • Look out of the front window at the horizon or a fixed point in the distance.
  • Avoid reading or looking at your phone. If you must, keep it brief and hold the screen up near eye level.
  • Closing your eyes, or listening to music or an audiobook, can help.

Food, drink and air

  • Eat a light meal before you go, and avoid heavy, greasy food and alcohol.
  • Open a window or turn on the air for cool, fresh air.

Ask a pharmacist

A pharmacist can recommend travel sickness medicines and explain when to take them, since many work best taken before the journey. See a GP if sickness is severe, new, or comes with other symptoms.

Learn your limits

Note each trip: seat, vehicle, what you were doing and how soon you felt sick. After a few trips you'll know what helps you most.
