What to do when you go blank mid-presentation

Almost everyone who speaks in front of people has felt their voice shake or their mind go empty. It feels huge from the inside, and it is usually much less obvious to the audience. A few habits make it less likely and much easier to recover from.

Before you speak

  • Rehearse out loud, not in your head. Say it at least three times, standing up.
  • Know your first two sentences by heart. Starting well calms the rest.
  • Write your talk as a short list of points, three words each, rather than a script you have to remember word for word.

If you go blank

  • Pause. A few seconds of silence feels long to you and normal to listeners.
  • Look at your three-word cue for the next point, and say it.
  • It is fine to say "Let me check my notes" or to repeat your last point in one sentence while you find the next one.

If your voice shakes

  • Slow down and lower your pace; shaking eases as your breathing slows.
  • Breathe out slowly before you start, longer than you breathe in.
  • Keep water nearby and take a sip between points.

Build up in steps

Practise alone, then record yourself, then speak to one person you trust, then a small group. Each step makes the next one feel smaller.

When to get help

If fear of speaking is stopping you from work, study or things you want to do, a doctor or therapist can help; there are effective approaches. Groups like local speaking clubs also offer friendly practice.
