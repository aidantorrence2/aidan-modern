Why your arms aren't growing even though you train them

Arms are a common sticking point. Often the cause is training that stopped progressing, too little direct work, not eating enough, or changing routines too often to see results. Genetics also play a part. This is general training information, not medical advice.

Common reasons for a stall

  • The weights and reps haven't increased for weeks.
  • Arms only get indirect work from presses and rows.
  • Not eating enough overall, or too little protein, to support muscle growth.
  • Changing exercises every week, so nothing gets time to work.

Train both sides of the arm

  • Triceps make up a large part of the upper arm, so include pressing and extension exercises as well as curls.
  • Pick two or three exercises for each and stick with them for a couple of months.

Make it progressive

  • Log weight and reps for every set.
  • When you hit the top of your rep range on every set with good form, add a little weight or a rep.
  • If you've stalled for three sessions, change one thing, not everything.

Track honestly

  • Measure your upper arm once a month the same way and take a photo in the same light.
  • Look at the trend over several months; arm size changes slowly.

Stay safe

Use controlled form rather than swinging the weight, and stop if a lift causes pain.
